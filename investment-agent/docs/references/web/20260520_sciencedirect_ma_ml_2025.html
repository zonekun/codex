Predicting mergers & acquisitions: A machine learning-based approach☆

Abstract

We provide empirical evidence on the predictability of Chinese merger and acquisition (M&A) activities by applying the machine learning approach in corporate finance studies to predict enterprises' M&A activities. We build a comprehensive set of 60 explanatory variables from the literature, employ a variety of widely used machine learning models to predict the occurrence of corporate acquisitions, and compare their predictive power with that of the traditional econometric method represented by the logit model. We show that machine learning has significant out-of-sample forecasting performance for takeovers compared to the logit model. In addition, we rank the importance of the variables and find that these important factors have a noticeable impact on the actual results of M&A prediction. Our findings indicate that utilising machine learning techniques to predict corporate takeover activities is effective and economically meaningful.

Introduction

In recent years, the global financial market has slowed down as a result of the severe shocks in the economic environment. With the gradual recovery of the global economy and many uncertainties as well as challenges ahead, the merger and acquisition (M&A) market has ushered in a “golden age”—a time when companies have greater opportunities to achieve high returns and even accelerate growth through deals. Therefore, enterprises can only survive in the face of fierce market competition by constantly developing and improving their competitiveness in a situation of rapid economic development, and M&A is one of the most common and critical ways for listed companies to expand externally (Ross, Westerfield, & Jordan, 2014). The ability to improve the long-term situation of both the merging and acquired companies by bringing in new resources when existing business model innovation has reached a bottleneck is the starting point of M&A investment, and takeovers also help them recognise their product structures, industry structures, and even corporate structures to make their businesses more synergistic. In addition, as an important tool for resource allocation and external governance, the value of takeovers is naturally reflected directly in the performance of subjects and, indirectly, in shareholder wealth. Wang, Dong, and Zhai (2023) further reinforced this perspective by demonstrating that companies experiencing goodwill impairment can enhance their investment efficiency by reducing overinvestment, and these firms exhibit notable improvements in the performance of subsequent M&A activities. These findings highlight how successful acquisition strategies significantly augment long-term worth for businesses and create considerable wealth growth for shareholders. However, a poor investment decision could result in massive losses and even pose a fatal threat to the survival of the company. Several studies have pointed out the widespread risk of failure in mergers (Steger & Kummer, 2007) and observed that the profitability of some companies even continues to decline in the years following acquisition completion (Magenheina & Mueller, 1988; Martynova, Oosting, & Renneboog, 2006). Chen et al. (2024) further emphasised the profound negative impact of M&A failures on corporate profitability and shareholder value. Hence, how to accurately predict M&A has become a major concern in both the theoretical and practical fields of the securities market.
In view of the importance and complexity of M&A forecasting, it has been a topic of focus and debate in the theoretical and practical fields of financial economics (Fama & French, 2000). Among them, the Market for Corporate Control (MCC) hypothesis, which is the theoretical foundation of takeover research, assumes that acquisition targets can be predicted using primary public accounting data (Manne, 1965), and this hypothesis opens up the way for subsequent studies. But the semi-strong-form Efficient Market Hypothesis (semi-strong-form EMH) proposed by Fama (1970) challenges this view and claims that stock prices already respond adequately to all publicly available information about a company's business prospects. That is to say, the predictability of M&A has become one of the key indicators for measuring the efficiency of the stock market. If takeovers could be predicted and the predicting outcomes could be used to guide investors (especially institutional investors with information advantages, such as security investment funds) to obtain excess returns, the semi-strong-form EMH would be rejected.
However, the accuracy of the listed company acquisition prediction has been extremely low in recent decades (Renneboog & Vansteenkiste, 2019), and this is largely attributed to the complex nature of M&A activity as well as limitations in research methods. The takeover likelihood is influenced by multi-dimensional factors, including firm level, deal level, management team level, industry level, country level, and others. But previous studies have tended to focus on the independent role of a single feature and lack a comprehensive investigation of the overall impact of these characteristics, resulting in insufficient comprehensiveness in acquisition prediction. Besides, takeover predictive models have faced challenges in their construction. Although these models, which are built using traditional econometric methods, attempt to forecast the probability of an attempted merger using publicly available information at the time of the announcement in order to help investors predict takeover activity and provide decision support, most early M&A models failed to demonstrate the desired predictive power as they claimed. Dietrich and Sorensen (1984), for example, used the logit model to predict acquisition targets and declared a prediction accuracy of up to 90 %, but this was questioned by many academics. Scholars such as Palepu (1986) and Espahbodi and Espahbodi (2003) pointed out that these takeover models suffer from sample selection bias, threshold setting irrationality, and other fatal flaws, leading to an overestimation of their predictive ability and could not accurately and robustly predict takeover targets. Therefore, there is a strong need to use more adaptable alternative methods to improve the accuracy of corporate M&A forecasting in the face of increasing scale, diversity, and potential non-linear relationships of data, as traditional methods are gradually revealing their limitations.
In this context, machine learning, as a representative artificial intelligence (AI) technology, is a good choice for predicting because it can perform classification and regression analysis by automatically looking for complicated structures and logical relationships in data and making judgements using specific algorithms (Jordan & Mitchell, 2015). Machine learning has been shown to be far superior to traditional econometric models in terms of predictive accuracy and to perform better when dealing with complex data and high-precision modelling problems (Charpentier, Flachaire, & Ly, 2018; Chen et al., 2019; Hastie, Tibshirani, & Friedman, 2009). It breaks through the limitations of traditional linear models and directly reveals more complicated relationships from the data, which provides stable prediction performance out-of-sample (Choudhury, Allen, & Endres, 2021). Finance has become the preferred landing scenario for technologies such as big data and AI due to its advantages of large and well-structured data reserves and high profits. Scholars have achieved many satisfactory results in applying machine learning to the financial field. Davis, Edelman, and Gammerman (1992) used machine learning algorithms in credit assessment to well predict applicant reliability and profitability, and Fitzpatrick and Mues (2016) successfully predicted mortgage default risk. Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) improved the measurement of risk premiums using methods like random forest. However, the application of machine learning to predict takeovers is still in its early stages of research with few successes, so it is critical to consider whether machine learning can be successfully applied to corporate acquisitions in the same way that “intelligent advisors” can.
This study focuses on M&A forecasting in the unique setting of the Chinese market for several reasons. Firstly, the merger market has been historically dominated by companies from developed countries. Foreign merger markets flourished after six waves of M&A, particularly in the late twentieth century, when the United States developed a mature acquisition market system (Faulkner, Teerikangas, & Joseph, 2012; Kleinert & Klodt, 2002; McCarthy & Dolfsma, 2012). But since the beginning of the twenty-first century, the global financial market has experienced a number of shocks, including the subprime mortgage crisis in late 2007, which resulted in a long-term negative impact on equity markets in the U.S. and Europe. The Chinese M&A market, on the other hand, has seized this opportunity and grown to become a major player in the global economy. China had become the world's second-largest M&A market by 2015, ranking only behind the U.S., with inflows into the domestic market through takeovers already reaching hundreds of billions of dollars (Black et al., 2015; Deng et al., 2017). Therefore, M&A activity in the Chinese capital market, as a major investor in developed economies, deserves further investigation. Secondly, the key and unique aspects of the Chinese financial system lie in its centrally controlled and bank-dominated nature (Leippold, Wang, & Zhou, 2022). The Chinese government intervenes in the financial market to a greater extent than other countries, especially in promoting merger activities of state-owned and leading enterprises (Lin & Bo, 2012; Wu, Wei, & Chen, 2020). In recent years, a number of policies have been introduced to strongly encourage takeovers of listed companies. The State Council of the People's Republic of China (PRC) issued “Opinions of the State Council on Promoting Enterprise Merger and Reorganisation” in 2010.1 In addition, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) published “Measures for the Administration of Material Assets Reorganisation of Listed Companies” in 20082 and recently amended it in 2023.3 By clarifying the significance of corporate acquisitions, promoting the reform of the examination and approval system, strengthening the guidance of industrial policies, and other active measures, the government's emphasis on takeovers has contributed to the sustained briskness of the M&A market in China. The number and scale of acquisition activities of Chinese listed companies have increased rapidly (Lebedev et al., 2015), making them a highly noteworthy and researchable group. Thirdly, although some deals do not create positive shareholder value (Goergen & Renneboog, 2004; Martynova, Oosting, & Renneboog, 2006), most of the quoted companies in the Chinese stock market are able to raise their share prices following M&A activities (Feng & Wu, 2001; Song & Duan, 2019). Hence, the increasingly mature merger market as well as frequent capital flows have prompted an increasing number of Chinese enterprises to seek profits from M&A as a means of investment. Leippold, Wang, and Zhou (2022) claim that one of the distinguishing features of the Chinese stock market is that it is dominated by retail investors, who have very clear short-term speculation. Therefore, broad investors and financial intermediaries pay close attention to these major investment decisions of listed companies that can generate considerable margins, so as to find investment opportunities and obtain substantial profits.
Based upon publicly available data, this paper uses machine learning techniques to conduct a relatively comprehensive study on predicting the likelihood of listed companies issuing acquisition announcements, with the aim of improving the prediction accuracy and recognising key factors that affect M&A decisions. We construct a high-dimensional corporate characteristics research indicator system containing a total of 60 factors, focusing on Chinese A-share listed companies from 2000 to 2022. When building these indices, we have followed the internationally recognised research framework and also fully considered the particularity of the Chinese market, incorporating as many potentially important variables as possible (Palepu, 1986). These variables cover multiple dimensions, such as enterprise earnings level, growth level, ownership structure, relevant personnel characteristics, as well as industry-related and environment-related features. In particular, we add several China-specific variables, for example, Post2013, which represents the special years, to explore the potential impact of changes in China's capital market on corporate takeover activities. Subsequently, as external investors, we investigate these characteristics' ability to forecast M&A activities. From the perspective of machine learning, this study is a typical supervised learning task, where a pattern is learnt or built by observing and analysing the given input and then predicting the output for any possible input values based on this pattern. Since numerous machine learning methods have different inherent principles, each has its own strengths and weaknesses, and they vary considerably between categories, this task adopts the most commonly used binary classification algorithms, namely the decision tree, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), Naïve Bayes, and K-nearest neighbour (KNN), to predict the release of takeover announcements and also seeks to find the more appropriate model to improve prediction accuracy. Meanwhile, we use a traditional logit model as the comparison benchmark.4 The results show that almost all of the machine learning methods have better out-of-sample prediction results than the logit model, with a prediction precision of 79 %. This promising result implies that it is feasible and effective to apply machine learning techniques to M&A prediction in the Chinese market. Besides, using the better-performing random forest model, we investigate the relative importance of input variables and identify that variables like acquisition experience, well-structured ownership, and director's and executive's compensation are notable factors influencing takeover forecasts. Further analysis also reveals that non-state-owned enterprises and firms with a high degree of equity dispersion have better forecasting performance, and that machine learning also demonstrates remarkable and robust predicting capability under different economic cycles and rolling forecasts close to real scenarios. We further provide evidence to support the considerable economic prospects brought by the M&A prediction.
This paper makes several contributions to the existing studies in this field. On the academic side, this paper enriches the research content in the field of M&A forecasting and broadens the research perspective to the Chinese market. First of all, regarding the application of machine learning techniques in predicting takeover activities, particularly in the unique and highly dynamic economic environment of China, our findings fill the gap in the existing literature to a certain extent. Although the existing studies of machine learning employed in the fields of finance and economics have been rapidly emerging, there is still a lack of predictive research for the Chinese M&A market, a secondary market, so this paper verifies the forecasting ability of machine learning techniques in the Chinese acquisition market, providing a new perspective to relevant literature. Second, by using machine learning algorithms to analyse multi-dimensional features, this paper not only explores the relative importance of each factor's predictive ability on acquisitions but also further studies the relationship between important features and the likelihood of merger activities. Compared to previous studies that only focused on single-dimensional characteristics, we more accurately capture the key factors affecting corporate takeover activities by constructing a series of more comprehensive acquisition predictors—60 in total—that are in line with the Chinese special characteristics, adding to the emerging literature on empirical M&A research in the Chinese market. Third, this paper significantly improves the accuracy of M&A forecasts, providing strong evidence for testing the semi-strong form of EMH, which makes substantial contributions to both financial theory research and practical applications. Last but not least, this paper is the first attempt to include all participants in takeover activities in the research scope, examining the M&A behaviour from a more comprehensive perspective and expanding the research boundaries of M&A prediction to capture the overall market trend. In fact, almost all existing research has been centred on the possibility of listed companies becoming merger targets. The majority of acquisition targets in the Chinese securities market are private enterprises; the lack of transparency in these non-listed firms' information makes it difficult for ordinary investors to obtain comprehensive and reliable information for accurate predictions. Therefore, this paper conducts research on a wider range of takeovers in the specific context of China and obtains conclusions that are more objective, neutral, and have much applicability, thereby providing a deeper understanding of the market and enhancing the applicability and practical value of M&A research.
This paper also has several practical implications. It is crucial for market participants to comprehend the possibility of any company becoming a participant in merger activities. The notable factors that affect acquisitions identified in this study can also help them better understand the dynamics and characteristics of the Chinese M&A market to make more sensible decisions, thus increasing market efficiency and promoting the healthy development of the market. For external investors and analysts, they can develop more precise and flexible investment strategies by predicting and looking into the listed firms that may release acquisition announcements in order to seize possible opportunities and address potential risks. By analysing the enterprise's own features as well as its role and possibility in the acquisition market, the company can evaluate the risks related to takeovers more effectively, so as to formulate merger tactics with high efficiency, better adaptability, and robust risk management strategies, and improve the rationality of resource allocation in the capital market. And at the same time, securities market regulators can take findings such as the non-linear relationship between characteristics and acquisitions as a basis for policy-making, which helps them better assess the strategic direction, market position, and potential risks of corporations, offering reference opinions for the development of a high-quality and normative M&A market in China.
The rest of the paper is organised as follows. Section 2 reviews the literature on machine learning and corporate finance forecasting and describes the factors that influence corporate takeovers. Section 3 describes our sample, input variables, and research methodology (Goodfellow, Bengio, & Courville, 2016; LeCun, Bengio, & Hinton, 2015; Zhou, 2021). Section 4 presents and discusses the empirical results and robustness tests. Section 5 exhibits the impact of takeovers on corporate performance. Finally, Section 6 concludes.
